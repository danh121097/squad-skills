# Design mindset, evaluation, and official sources

Use for ambiguous art direction, critique, design validation and current platform guidance.

## Designer mindset

- Start from product purpose, content, user context and existing system; style is a consequence.
- Distinguish novelty from differentiation and familiarity from generic output.
- Prefer one strong design thesis with coherent constraints over a collage of trends.
- Design systems reduce repeated decisions; they do not erase product-specific hierarchy.
- Evaluate the whole flow over isolated hero frames and screenshots.
- Motion is information in time: spatial relationship, causality, feedback and continuity.
- Treat accessibility, performance, localization and implementation cost as design materials.
- Preserve verified user/product decisions; make trade-offs visible when evidence conflicts.

## Evaluation passes

1. **Comprehension:** can the intended user identify purpose, state and next action quickly?
2. **Task:** can the full job complete, recover and resume with realistic data/permissions/errors?
3. **Hierarchy:** do type, spacing, contrast and grouping match information priority?
4. **Consistency:** do components/tokens/interactions form one system without flattening distinct roles?
5. **Accessibility:** keyboard/touch/screen reader/zoom/reduced motion and cognitive clarity.
6. **Responsive:** content, navigation and actions remain usable across actual supported contexts.
7. **Craft:** alignment, rhythm, optical balance, typography, imagery, states and motion are intentional.
8. **Implementation:** maps to repository primitives with known behavior/performance and bounded additions.

Use heuristic review, cognitive walkthrough, task-based usability testing, prototype testing or analytics
according to risk. Record evidence and unresolved assumptions; do not claim user validation from expert
critique alone.
